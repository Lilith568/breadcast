Bake what sells. Waste less.

Small bakeries guess tomorrow's quantities from yesterday's gut feeling, and the unsold rest is thrown away. Breadcast learns demand from your own sales history, right in the browser, and tells you how much to bake per product.

Breadcast does not just predict the average. It picks the quantity that balances a lost sale against a wasted item (the newsvendor rule). Enter prices and costs per item.

How we measure success

Breadcast is judged by two numbers each week: the waste rate (unsold items ÷ baked items) and the sold-out rate (days a product ran out before closing). Success means a lower waste rate over four weeks without a higher sold-out rate or lower margin than the four weeks before. The backtest above is the same measurement applied to the past.

How it works

  • Model: a linear quantile regression on log sales per product, with weekday, season, holiday, weather and recent sales features, trained with Adam on the pinball loss directly in your browser.
  • Censored demand: when everything was sold, true demand was higher than sales. Those days only penalise under-prediction, so the model does not learn that sell-outs mean "low demand".
  • Decision: the quantile is the newsvendor critical ratio (price − cost) ÷ (price − value if unsold + extra cost of waste). Making waste more expensive lowers the target and the bake quantity.

The sample bakery is synthetic. Its "actually baked" column follows a common rule of thumb (average of the last four same weekdays plus 20%). Results on real data will differ.
